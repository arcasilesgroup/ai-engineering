{ai} research · 002 — do the surfaces actually have a goal loop?

Measured 2026-09-11 on this machine, against the binaries installed here. The question is the one ai-goal rests on: can the surface be handed a goal and left to iterate, or does the honest path stay the human loop of blueprint §20.1?

00Method

The primary source is the installed binary itself, not documentation about it: each surface's own --help, read at the version on this machine. A surface counts as native when the binary advertises a built-in mode that (a) takes an objective, (b) executes without a human turn per step, and (c) iterates until it stops on its own. Anything short of that is none — and a surface that could not be measured here stays unverified rather than being guessed.

for c in claude opencode codex copilot pi omp cursor-agent; do
  printf '%s ' "$c"; command -v $c >/dev/null && $c --version | head -1 || echo "not installed"
done
# then, per surface: $c --help, read for the mode that grants an objective unattended

Every one of the eight surfaces was installed here, so nothing is left at unverified by absence of the tool.

02What this means for ai-goal

Six surfaces keep contract mode: the contract is pinned and their own loop executes it. Pi and Zed keep facilitator mode: ai-goal walks plan.html one step at a time with the human as the clock — and Pi is the interesting case, because it is the one surface this project governs in-process while its core still ships no loop of its own. A guard and a loop are different capabilities, and a surface can have the first without the second.

03What this does not prove

It is documented capability, not a run. Each verdict rests on the installed binary advertising the mode, captured verbatim above. It does not rest on having driven a full autonomous loop to completion on each surface — that would need a real objective per surface, model access, and a budget, and pretending otherwise would be the optimism this field exists to refuse. The claim is exactly what was measured: the mode exists at this version.

Versions move. The evidence lines name the version measured, so a later release that removes a flag is a delta against a recorded claim rather than against somebody's memory — and the registry test refuses a native or none verdict that carries no evidence at all.
