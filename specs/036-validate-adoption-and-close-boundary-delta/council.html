## Round three — the chairman

No lens names in this room; the spec and both rounds only.

**What the lenses agree on.** The table is real. Eight rows, eight modules, eight contract symbols, all present; evidence.py is 445 lines as printed; the dates 2026-08-13 and 2026-08-14 are in the git history; cost.py and decision_fw.py carry the cited research references. The central claim — that eight of the nine behaviours already ship — is the best-verified sentence in the record, and it reproduces under command. The red-first disclaimer is present and the not-yet claims are true: the two test files do not exist, the harness runs green at skilleval=363 against a margin-0 baseline that the corpus rows must move, and no module named decision_boundary exists to collide with. Avoiding the bare word *boundary* is the right instinct.

**Where they clash.** Two arguments run through the rounds. The first: how much of the undecidable path must be settled in this record — one side reads the overloaded `None` as a dropped reason that will bite the first caller, the other reads the report string as the caller's to enrich and the manifest validation as the guard that makes the empty case unreachable. The second: how much corpus mechanics the record must specify before the plan runs — one side holds that B-036-2's prescription points at the wrong file for the shape it names, the refutations hold that the fixtures-first plan will discover and correct whatever the prose misplaces, so the cost of the mismatch is a plan-time correction rather than a broken contract.

**Blind spots the cross-read caught.** The parse-surface mismatch — the `Not for … — …` shape is a SKILL.md-description shape, and placing it in corpus.md makes the tail inert while the quoted shell moves the baseline — was invisible to every lens that read the regexes without reading the file the rule lands in. The provenance half of the table lies outside the freshness test's asserted surface, and "documented responsibility" has no defined test input. And the corpus example is the only one without a red side.

**Verdict.** A record that is honest about what exists and what does not, whose load-bearing claims reproduce under command, and whose earlier gaps are mostly closed: the per-row symbols land, the red-first disclaimer lands, 035's bytes are untouched, and the name avoids the collision even though the count behind it is wrong. It is not yet complete in three measurable places: the supersession link is described but not written (this record names no digest by value, and 035's side carries no note); the corpus rule is prescribed at a location whose shape the harness does not read; and the freshness check leaves half of its own table out of scope. Each of the three is a plan-time deliverable, which is why the record survives — and none of the three should survive as prose only.

**Recommendation.** Make the three promised artifacts first-class plan tasks with red-first outcomes of their own: the record that names 035's digests by value, the note on 035's record, and — before either — the relocation of B-036-2's prescription to the surface the harness actually parses: a `Not for … — …` refusal in the SKILL.md descriptions if the rule is to ride the refusals lane, or a quoted corpus case with a named destination if it is to be a case at all. In the same commit, add the provenance column (dates and citations) to what the freshness test asserts, and give "documented responsibility" a checkable shape.

**One first step.** Run the smallest experiment before the plan fixes the three corpus rows: add one `Not for decisions outside the declared boundary — blocked, never routed.` refusal to one SKILL.md description and one quoted boundary case to one `corpus.md`, run `uv run python tests/skill_eval.py`, and read which lane counts which — that one output settles whether B-036-2's placement is a copyedit or a correction.
